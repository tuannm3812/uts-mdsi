# 36127 iLab Project Kickoff Requirements — Spring 2026

Source: `36127-ilab-project-kickoff-slides-spring-2026.pdf` (28 slides)

## Project structure

- Complete a defined piece of work that applies knowledge from previous subjects.
- Produce substantial or innovative work with mentor guidance.
- Document, report, and present the project for an academic and/or professional audience.
- Group size: **5–6 students**.
- Expected effort: **at least 9 hours per student per week for 12 weeks**.
- Mentor meeting: approximately **30 minutes each week** during tutorials.
- The mentor should contact the group by the end of the first week.

## Assessments

| Component | Weight | Due | Indicative length / format |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Project Proposal | 20% | Week 5 | Approximately 15 pages |
| Progress Report | 10% | Week 9 | Approximately 15 pages |
| Presentation | 20% | Week 11 | 15-minute recording and 15-minute Q&A |
| Final Report | 50% | Week 12 | Approximately 50 pages, excluding appendices |

All assessment components are mandatory and submitted through Canvas. Templates are provided in Canvas.

> [!WARNING]
> Slides 8–11 describe the proposal, progress report, presentation, and final report as **group work**, but slide 12 labels them **individual**. Confirm the authoritative arrangement in Canvas or with the coordinator before planning responsibilities.

## Weekly individual reports

Weekly reports do not add separate assessment marks. They are used to calculate an **Individual Contribution Factor** that can reduce a student's group assessment mark.

Each one-page report should document:

- Tasks completed that week
- Individual role and responsibilities
- Contribution to overall progress
- Challenges encountered
- Plan for the following week
- Links to evidence, such as GitHub commits, Google Docs history, meeting minutes, Slack/Teams messages, analysis, notebooks, or code

### Contribution criteria

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Completion of assigned tasks | 30% |
| Quality and quantity of supporting evidence | 35% |
| Participation in meetings and team communication | 20% |
| Reflection, planning, and professionalism | 15% |

### Reporting stages

| Stage | Weekly reports | Applied to |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Weeks 2, 3, and 4 | Proposal |
| 2 | Weeks 6, 7, and 8 | Progress Report |
| 3 | Weeks 9, 10, and 11 | Final Report |

Within each stage, the first two reports are worth 30 contribution-score points each and the third is worth 40.

### Contribution factor

| Contribution score | Factor |
|---:|---:|
| 90–100 | 1.00 |
| 80–89 | 0.95 |
| 70–79 | 0.90 |
| 60–69 | 0.85 |
| 50–59 | 0.80 |
| Below 50 | Teaching-team review |

## Collaboration requirements

- Use **Slack**, not email, for group and mentor communication.
- Add the mentor to the project Slack workspace/channel.
- Keep communication professional and visible because it may be used as contribution evidence.
- Suggested Slack channels: meeting minutes, resources, project discussions, and issue tracking.
- Manage **all code contributions with Git**.
- Add the mentor to the Git project/group.
- Use the UTS student email address when registering the project account.
- Track work proactively through tasks, a diary, issues, or another visible project-management system.

## Submission rules

- Assessments are checked with similarity-detection tools.
- The slides state that the similarity score must not exceed **35%**.
- Do not copy material from previously submitted reports, including your own.
- Late penalty: **5% of the maximum available mark per calendar day**.
- A submission more than seven calendar days late receives zero.

## Items to verify in Canvas

- Whether the four major assessments are submitted and marked as group or individual work
- Exact templates and marking rubrics
- Current due dates and submission times
- Whether the stated 35% similarity threshold has qualifications or exclusions
- Weekly-report submission location and exact template
- Presentation scheduling and attendance requirements
- Final project allocation and mentor contact
