## Evaluation-inflation risk — now confirmed multiple times independently, not a one-off claim

This is the single most important finding of this scan, and it directly matters for how much to trust any headline number in this literature, including our own future results.

- **FireSenseNet's own authors** report that common evaluation shortcuts inflate reported F1 by **over 44%**.
- **On the exact same "Next Day Wildfire Spread" benchmark dataset**, the systematic review (Andrianarivony & Akhloufi 2024) tabulates wildly different results across papers: U-Net+attention F1 = 36%; Multi-kernel CNN F1 = 70.97%; CNN-ASPP F1 = 97%; ASUFM (Swin-Transformer) PR-AUC = 37–39%. **Same data, same task, a 60-point F1 spread.** Architecture choice cannot plausibly explain that; evaluation/validation design almost certainly can.
- **A specific, named instance:** Shadrin et al. (2024, *Scientific Reports*)'s MA-Net — the exact method the Dubey & Dubey MethodsX paper says it adapts — is independently reported (in the same MDPI review) at **F1 = 64–68%** on a 1–5 day horizon over Russian fire data. The Dubey & Dubey paper, extending that same method with an added FRBS layer on a different dataset, reports **F1 = 92.9%**. A jump of that size on a closely related method, without a correspondingly large architectural change, is exactly the pattern FireSenseNet's authors warned about.

**Implication for us:** this project already learned, the hard way, that a headline match-rate number can be almost entirely an artifact of reference-data scale and validation design rather than a real capability (the 17.1%-vs-97.12% NSW-RFS-vs-NPWS finding). This literature confirms that's not a one-off lesson specific to our pilot — it's a widespread, largely unaddressed problem in exactly the sub-field we're now entering. Any number we report for Option B needs to explicitly state its validation design (random split vs. chronological vs. spatial vs. split-complex) alongside the metric, or it's not comparable to anything.

## Recurring patterns across this literature

1. **Cross-attention/multimodal fusion is already common** — FireSenseNet, FireRisk-Multi, AUBLSTM, and both Dubey/Dubey papers all do some form of it. A transformer with cross-attention fusion, by itself, is not a novel claim — same lesson as the original detection-literature scan back in Week 1.
2. **Uncertainty quantification is treated as a bolt-on to the *model*, not the *labels*.** Every uncertainty paper found (epistemic/aleatoric, conformal prediction, Deep Ensembles, boundary-aware, ECE/MCE) quantifies uncertainty in the model's prediction, assuming the training labels (fire events, hotspot ground truth) are clean. None of them audit the reliability of the reference labels themselves before training on them.
3. **Evaluation leakage/inflation is real, acknowledged, and now confirmed multiple independent ways** — not a hypothetical risk, a measured one, in this exact literature (see dedicated section above).
4. **Geography is skewed toward North America and Russia/China.** TS-SatFire (CONUS), the Canadian Fire Spread Dataset, WildfireSpreadTS, BCWildfire (boreal), Shadrin et al. (Russia), AutoST-Net (China) — none of the closely comparable forecasting papers found, including across the 37-study systematic review, are Australian.

## Where the innovation angle actually is

Not in "transformer + cross-attention" (crowded) or "add uncertainty" (crowded) alone. Three concrete, evidence-backed angles that build directly on the Phase 1 work already completed, rather than treating it as sunk cost:

1. **Label-confidence-aware forecasting, not just model-uncertainty-aware forecasting.** Every UQ paper found quantifies *model* uncertainty over *assumed-clean* labels. This project already has a working reference-confidence pipeline (the reliability audit) that classifies hotspots as confirmed/unresolved/non-fire rather than a forced binary. Propagating that label-confidence into the forecasting model's training and its own uncertainty estimate — rather than treating the label as ground truth — is a genuine, defensible gap, and it directly reuses this week's work instead of discarding it.
2. **Split-complex, hold-out-event validation as a first-class requirement, not an afterthought, with explicit validation-design reporting.** This is now backed by three independent pieces of evidence, not one: FireSenseNet's 44% inflation claim, the systematic review's 60-point F1 spread on identical data, and the Shadrin-vs-Dubey discrepancy. This project independently found the same failure mode (Kerry Ridge/Gospers Mountain dominating ~98% of matches) before finding any of this literature. Building the forecasting evaluation around split-complex holdouts, and reporting validation design alongside every metric, is a real, now well-evidenced methodological contribution — arguably the strongest single argument to bring to Arnick.
3. **Australian/NSW case study.** None of the closely comparable forecasting papers, including across a 37-study systematic review, are Australian — geographic transfer of these methods to Australian fire regimes and data infrastructure (DEA Hotspots, NPWS, Digital Atlas burnt-area layer) is itself under-explored in what's been found so far.

## Recommended minimum viable design for Option B

Following `research-execution-plan-2026.md`'s phase structure and this scan's findings:

- **Target:** hotspot presence/probability at 1–7 day horizon (multi-horizon output preferred over a single fixed day, per Arnick's own framing and consistent with the Uncertainty-Aware DL paper's finding that aleatoric uncertainty grows with horizon — worth showing that growth explicitly).
- **Labels:** three-class (confirmed / unresolved / confirmed non-fire), reusing the reliability-audit pipeline already built, not a forced binary — directly extends T-009's finding.
- **Architecture:** transformer with cross-attention fusion across a hotspot-history branch, a weather branch, and a vegetation/land-cover branch, following TS-SatFire's task framing and FireSenseNet's cross-attention-gate design as concrete templates. AUBLSTM (attention-BiLSTM) as a lighter fallback if a full transformer is too heavy for the semester/compute budget.
- **Uncertainty:** start with Deep Ensembles or MC Dropout (cheaper to implement than a fully Bayesian model) for epistemic uncertainty; consider conformal prediction (per the already-known 2207.13250 paper) for a distribution-free coverage guarantee on top; report ECE/MCE alongside F1/IoU, per the IEEE Access paper's metric suite.
- **Explainability:** attention-weight inspection plus a simple rule-based/fuzzy interpretation layer over the top predictors, following the Mamdani FRBS design worked out in full in the MethodsX paper (fuzzification → rule base → min-max inference → centroid defuzzification → action mapping) — a genuinely reusable template independent of that paper's own questionable headline accuracy.
- **Validation:** chronological + split-complex + leave-region-out holdouts, explicitly informed by this project's own concentration finding and now three independent literature confirmations of the same risk. State the validation design next to every reported metric, always.
