## Recurring patterns across this literature

1. **Cross-attention/multimodal fusion is already common** — FireSenseNet, FireRisk-Multi, AUBLSTM, and the fuzzy-transformer paper all do some form of it. A transformer with cross-attention fusion, by itself, is not a novel claim — same lesson as the original detection-literature scan back in Week 1.
2. **Uncertainty quantification is treated as a bolt-on to the *model*, not the *labels*.** Every uncertainty paper found (epistemic/aleatoric, conformal prediction, Deep Ensembles, boundary-aware) quantifies uncertainty in the model's prediction, assuming the training labels (fire events, hotspot ground truth) are clean. None of them audit the reliability of the reference labels themselves before training on them.
3. **Evaluation leakage is a real, acknowledged problem, not a theoretical one.** FireSenseNet's own authors report that naive evaluation setups inflate F1 by over 44%. This is the same class of problem this project already found empirically (event concentration, hold-out-event necessity) — not a hypothetical risk, a measured one in comparable work.
4. **Geography is skewed toward North America.** TS-SatFire (CONUS), the Canadian Fire Spread Dataset, WildfireSpreadTS, BCWildfire (boreal) — none of the closely comparable forecasting papers found are Australian.

## Where the innovation angle actually is

Not in "transformer + cross-attention" (crowded) or "add uncertainty" (crowded) alone. Three concrete, evidence-backed angles that build directly on the Phase 1 work already completed, rather than treating it as sunk cost:

1. **Label-confidence-aware forecasting, not just model-uncertainty-aware forecasting.** Every UQ paper found quantifies *model* uncertainty over *assumed-clean* labels. This project already has a working reference-confidence pipeline (the reliability audit) that classifies hotspots as confirmed/unresolved/non-fire rather than a forced binary. Propagating that label-confidence into the forecasting model's training and its own uncertainty estimate — rather than treating the label as ground truth — is a genuine, defensible gap, and it directly reuses this week's work instead of discarding it.
2. **Split-complex, hold-out-event validation as a first-class requirement, not an afterthought.** FireSenseNet's own authors found evaluation shortcuts inflate F1 by 44%; this project independently found the same failure mode (Kerry Ridge/Gospers Mountain dominating ~98% of matches) before finding that paper. Building the forecasting evaluation around split-complex holdouts from the start, informed by concrete NSW-specific evidence rather than a generic best practice, is a real methodological contribution.
3. **Australian/NSW case study.** None of the closely comparable forecasting papers are Australian — geographic transfer of these methods to Australian fire regimes and data infrastructure (DEA Hotspots, NPWS, Digital Atlas burnt-area layer) is itself under-explored in what's been found so far.

## Recommended minimum viable design for Option B

Following `research-execution-plan-2026.md`'s phase structure and this scan's findings:

- **Target:** hotspot presence/probability at 1–7 day horizon (multi-horizon output preferred over a single fixed day, per Arnick's own framing and consistent with the Uncertainty-Aware DL paper's finding that aleatoric uncertainty grows with horizon — worth showing that growth explicitly).
- **Labels:** three-class (confirmed / unresolved / confirmed non-fire), reusing the reliability-audit pipeline already built, not a forced binary — directly extends T-009's finding.
- **Architecture:** transformer with cross-attention fusion across a hotspot-history branch, a weather branch, and a vegetation/land-cover branch, following TS-SatFire's task framing and FireSenseNet's cross-attention-gate design as concrete templates. AUBLSTM (attention-BiLSTM) as a lighter fallback if a full transformer is too heavy for the semester/compute budget.
- **Uncertainty:** start with Deep Ensembles or MC Dropout (cheaper to implement than a fully Bayesian model) for epistemic uncertainty; consider conformal prediction (per the already-known 2207.13250 paper) for a distribution-free coverage guarantee on top.
- **Explainability:** attention-weight inspection plus a simple rule-based/fuzzy interpretation layer over the top predictors, per the transformer+fuzzy-inference paper's design, rather than relying on attention weights alone (which are not inherently faithful explanations — a lesson already flagged in this project's original literature review).
- **Validation:** chronological + split-complex + leave-region-out holdouts, explicitly informed by this project's own concentration finding and FireSenseNet's evaluation-leakage warning.
